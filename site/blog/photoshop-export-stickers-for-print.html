How to Export Stickers from Photoshop for Print

GuidePhotoshop4 min readUpdated 20 October 2026

You hit Export, choose PNG, and send it off. A day later the printer writes back: "low resolution", "needs CMYK", "no bleed". Exporting stickers from Photoshop isn't hard, but the right file depends on what happens next: making the cut line, or printing.

What I find genuinely useful is splitting it into two jobs. Once you see it that way, choosing the format becomes obvious.

Two jobs, two files

JobBest fileWhy
Making the cut line (a cutting tool, CorelDraw trace)Transparent PNGThe transparency defines the sticker's shape

Step 1: Set the size and resolution

PrintKeg's setup guide asks for raster artwork of at least 300 DPI at the final print size, and that's a good rule for any sticker.

  1. Choose Image, then Image Size.
  2. Set the width and height in millimetres or inches to the real sticker size.
  3. Check that Resolution reads 300 pixels/inch. If you have to turn Resample on to get there, the image is being enlarged. That can look soft, so start from a bigger original if you can.

A quick reality check: a 75 mm (2.95 in) sticker at 300 ppi needs about 886 pixels across.

Step 2: Export a transparent PNG for the cut line

  1. Hide any background layer so the area around the sticker is transparent.
  2. Choose File, then Export, then Export As, pick PNG, and keep transparency on.

Here's something most people don't know: PNG can't be CMYK. Adobe's file format page describes PNG as preserving transparency in grayscale and RGB images, and an Adobe staff member explains that PNG was designed for the web and doesn't support CMYK. If your document is CMYK, Export As converts it to RGB on the way out, so the PNG is always RGB.

That's fine for a cut line tool, which only needs the shape. The free Die Line Maker reads the transparency, traces the outline, and gives you a CorelDraw-ready PDF.

Step 3: Save a print-ready PDF

Printer guides from 4OVER4 describe the standard route:

  1. Choose File, then Save As and pick Photoshop PDF.
  2. Start from the [Press Quality] preset.
  3. Set the standard to PDF/X-1a if your printer asks for it.
  4. Turn on trim marks and bleed if the printer wants them, then save.

If the printer wants CMYK, convert with Image, then Mode, then CMYK Color before saving, and check colours on screen first. Bright RGB oranges and blues can shift in CMYK.

Step 4: Add bleed (Photoshop has no bleed setting)

4OVER4 points out that Photoshop has no built-in bleed setting, so you add it yourself:

  • Choose Image, then Canvas Size and add twice your bleed to the width and height (for 3 mm bleed, add 6 mm).
  • Extend the background or white border into that extra space.
  • Drag guides to mark the trim edge.

Paperlust uses 3 mm of bleed beyond the cut; PrintKeg asks for at least 0.125 in (3.2 mm). Our bleed and safe zone guide has the full table.

A quick pre-send checklist

  • 300 ppi at the final size
  • Transparent PNG for the cut line, RGB
  • PDF (or PSD/TIFF) for print, CMYK if asked
  • About 3 mm of bleed
  • Text and logos at least 2 to 3 mm inside the cut

What this means for you

The confusion around Photoshop exports usually comes from asking one file to do two jobs. Give the cutting step a transparent PNG and the printer a proper PDF, and the problems disappear.

The takeaway

To export stickers from Photoshop for print, set 300 ppi at the final size, export a transparent RGB PNG for the cut line, and save a Press Quality PDF with bleed for the printer. Two files, two jobs, and no more emails about low resolution.

Questions people ask

Can I save a PNG in CMYK from Photoshop?

No. PNG supports RGB and grayscale with transparency, not CMYK. Use PDF, PSD or TIFF for CMYK print files.

What resolution should stickers be?

300 ppi (DPI) at the final print size. A 75 mm sticker needs about 886 pixels across.

How do I add bleed in Photoshop?

Photoshop has no bleed setting. Use Image, then Canvas Size to add twice the bleed, then extend the background into the new space.
