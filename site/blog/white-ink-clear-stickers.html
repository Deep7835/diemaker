White Ink for Clear Stickers: How to Set Up RDG_WHITE

TutorialCorelDraw5 min readUpdated 20 October 2026

Clear stickers look amazing on a laptop or a glass door. Then the first test print comes out and the colours look pale, almost ghostly, because the light passes straight through the ink. The fix is white ink for clear stickers: a layer of white printed under your colours so they stay bright.

Here's the good news. Setting this up is mostly about one named spot colour. Once your file has it, the printer knows exactly where to lay down white. Let me walk you through it.

Why clear stickers need white ink

Normal printing relies on white paper. Cyan, magenta, yellow and black inks are slightly see-through, and the white paper behind them makes the colours glow. Take away the white paper and the colours lose their punch.

That's why printers add a white layer on clear and metallic material. One sticker printer's artwork setup guide puts it simply: a white vector layer should back up the colour areas, and on silver stock the white is printed first, with CMYK on top.

What RDG_WHITE is

RDG_WHITE is the spot colour name Roland's VersaWorks RIP uses for white ink. Roland's UV printing guide explains that the parts of your file set to RDG_WHITE are the parts VersaWorks prints with white ink.

It works just like CutContour. The RIP doesn't care what RDG_WHITE looks like on screen. It cares that the swatch has that exact name.

Check your printer modelRoland's VersaWorks settings article notes that this white ink feature applies to specific white-ink printers. Check that your machine supports white ink before you plan a job around it.

Full white or design-only white?

You have two good choices, and each one suits a different look.

Full white (whole sticker)Design-only white
What gets whiteEverything inside the cut lineOnly the printed design
Clear areasNone, the sticker looks solidStay see-through
Best forBright, opaque stickers on clear vinylLogos that "float" on glass or a laptop
Holes in the designFilled with whiteStay clear

Full white is the safer start. Design-only white is where clear stickers really shine, because the gaps in the design show the surface behind it.

How to set up RDG_WHITE in CorelDraw

  1. Load Roland's palette. Roland's VersaWorks 7 manual explains how to download the CorelDRAW palette data and load it through Window, then Color Palettes, then Palettes. The RDG spot colours, including RDG_White, live in the userinks.xml file.
  2. Make the white shape. Duplicate your design outline (or the cut shape for full white) onto a new layer named RDG_WHITE.
  3. Fill it with RDG_WHITE. Apply the spot colour as a fill, with no outline.
  4. Set it to overprint. Right-click and tick Overprint Fill, so the white sits under your colours instead of knocking them out.
  5. Export with spot colours kept. Publish to PDF and make sure spot colours are not converted to CMYK.

Roland's guide for Illustrator does the same thing a slightly different way: it duplicates the colour layer, renames the copy "White" and fills it with RDG_WHITE. The idea is identical in CorelDraw.

What if your file has no white layer?

This is genuinely useful to know. Roland's UV guide says VersaWorks can generate a white ink plate from data that doesn't include RDG_WHITE. The catch, according to the same guide, is that it can't make a white plate for just one specific object.

So the automatic option is handy for simple jobs. For design-only white, or anything precise, a real RDG_WHITE layer in your file gives you control.

Tips for clean results

  • Keep the cut line separate. White ink is a fill on its own layer. CutContour stays a thin outline on its own layer.
  • Use good resolution. PrintKeg asks for 300 DPI at the final size, and a white layer traced from a sharp image has cleaner edges.
  • Mind the edges. Ask your printer whether they want the white shrunk very slightly so it never peeks out past the colour (sometimes called a choke). Machines and shops handle this differently.
  • Print a test. White ink density and order vary by machine. One test sticker answers most questions.

The takeaway

White ink for clear stickers comes down to one named layer under your colours. Use full white for bold, solid stickers and design-only white for that floating look. Name it RDG_WHITE for Roland, set it to overprint, keep spot colours on export, and print one test. You'll get bright colours on clear vinyl, and you'll know exactly why it works.

Questions people ask

What is RDG_WHITE?

It's the spot colour name Roland VersaWorks uses for white ink. Areas of your file set to RDG_WHITE are printed with white ink.

Should white ink go under the whole sticker?

For solid, bright stickers, yes. For a logo that floats on glass, put white only under the design so the gaps stay clear.

Do all printers support white ink?

No. Only printers with white ink can print it, and the spot colour name can differ between brands. Check with your printer before you design the file.
