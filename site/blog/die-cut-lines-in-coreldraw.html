What a die cut line actually is

A die cut line is a thin vector path that tells a machine where to cut. It sits on top of your artwork, but it is never printed. The cutter, the RIP or the die maker reads it as an instruction.

A RIP is the software that sits between your file and the printer. Roland's VersaWorks is a common one. It looks at your PDF, finds the cut path by its colour name, and sends that path to the blade.

That's why the details matter. According to the CorelDraw guide on freecdr.in, the cut path must be one closed shape with no stray nodes or overlapping segments. A tiny gap in the path can stop the cutter or send it in a strange direction.

What you need before you start

Let me explain the checklist first. It makes every step after this one easier.

  • A clean design. A PNG with a transparent background or a vector logo works best.
  • The final size. Know the sticker size in mm or inches, for example 75 mm (2.95 in) wide.
  • Your printer's specs. Ask for their cut line colour name, line width, bleed and safe zone. They differ between shops.
  • Enough resolution. PrintKeg's setup guide asks for at least 300 DPI at the final print size. That's a good rule for any sticker.

Step 1: Put the cut line on its own layer

Open the Objects docker (Window, then Docker, then Objects). Create a new layer and name it something clear, like CutContour or DieLine.

Keep this layer above your artwork layers. The freecdr.in guide recommends exactly this: a separate layer for the cut line, sitting on top of the print. It keeps the cut path easy to find, lock and check.

TipLock your artwork layer while you work on the cut line. It stops you from nudging the design by accident.

Step 2: Draw or trace the cut path

This is the step most people worry about. There are two good ways to do it.

Option A: Trace it inside CorelDraw

  1. Select your bitmap and choose Bitmaps, then Outline Trace, then Logo (or Line Art). CorelDraw creates vector shapes from the image.
  2. Select the traced shapes and Weld them into one shape (Object, then Shaping, then Weld).
  3. Open the Contour tool (Ctrl + F9 in most versions). Choose Outside, one step, and an offset of about 2 mm (0.08 in).
  4. Break the contour apart (Ctrl + K), delete the inner shape, and keep the outer outline.
  5. Use the Shape tool (F10) to remove extra nodes and smooth sharp points.

Menu names move around a little between CorelDraw versions, so if a command isn't where I say, search for it with the Help search box.

Step 3: Give it a spot colour and a thin stroke

This is where it gets interesting. The cutter doesn't care what colour the line looks like. It cares what the colour is called.

A spot colour is a named colour that stays separate from your CMYK print. For Roland machines, the standard name is CutContour, spelled exactly like that. Paperlust's setup guide says the same thing: use a 100% magenta spot colour on the top layer and confirm the swatch name with your supplier.

  1. Open the Palette Editor (Window, then Color Palettes).
  2. Add a new colour, set its type to Spot, and name it CutContour.
  3. Apply it to the outline of your cut path.
  4. Set the outline width to 0.25 pt (0.09 mm), or a hairline if your printer asks for one.

Roland also offers CorelDRAW palette files for VersaWorks. Their manual explains how to load them from Window, then Color Palettes, then Palettes. That saves you from typing names by hand.

Why 0.25 pt? Paperlust asks for a hairline of 0.25 pt or thinner, and the Akron-Summit County Public Library's cut line tutorial uses 0.25 pt too. freecdr.in mentions a range of 0.25 pt to 1 pt. When in doubt, ask.

Step 4: No fill, and set the outline to overprint

This part is actually really easy. Click the cut path and set the fill to None. The cut line should only be an outline.

Then right-click it and tick Overprint Outline. Overprint means the line sits on top without punching a white gap into the colours below. Paperlust's guide gives the same advice for the same reason.

Step 5: Check bleed and the safe zone

Bleed is extra artwork that runs past the cut line. A safe zone is a margin inside the cut where text and logos stay.

Paperlust uses 3 mm (0.12 in) of bleed and keeps key artwork at least 2 mm (0.08 in) inside the cut. PrintKeg asks for 0.125 in (3.2 mm) for both. Turn on View, then Bleed in CorelDraw to see if your artwork really runs past the line.

I wrote a full guide on this, with a quick reference table: sticker bleed and safe zone.

Step 6: Export a PDF that keeps the spot colour

You're almost there. Choose File, then Publish to PDF (or Export, then PDF). Open the settings and go to the Color tab.

Untick Convert spot colours to CMYK. The freecdr.in guide flags this as the setting that breaks most files. If the spot colour is converted, the RIP can't find your cut line.

Finally, reopen the PDF and check that the cut line is still a spot colour. In Acrobat, the Output Preview panel lists every separation. You should see CutContour there.

What this means for you

Here's the thing. Most cut line problems aren't about skill. They come from one missed setting: a wrong name, a converted colour, or a missing bleed.

So build yourself a small habit. Name the layer, name the spot colour, set 0.25 pt, tick overprint, check bleed, keep spot colours on export. Six checks, every file.

Based on what printers' own setup guides keep repeating, the files that never need a reprint aren't the fanciest ones. They're the ones where those six things were checked every single time.

The short version

Making die cut lines in CorelDraw comes down to a clean closed path, a correctly named spot colour, a thin overprinting stroke, and a PDF that keeps that colour intact. Get those right and any printer or cutter will read your file without a phone call.

Questions people ask

Which colour should a die cut line be in CorelDraw?

Use a spot colour, not a CMYK colour. For Roland VersaWorks the standard name is CutContour, usually shown as 100% magenta. Other printers may use another name, so ask them and type it exactly.

How thick should a die cut line be?

Most sticker printers ask for 0.25 pt (0.09 mm) or a hairline. Some die makers use thicker lines, for example 1 pt or 2 pt for packaging. Your printer's spec always wins.

Can I make a die cut line from a JPG?

Yes. Trace the image in CorelDraw with Outline Trace, or upload it to Die Line Maker, which removes a plain background and traces the outline for you. A higher resolution image gives a cleaner path.
