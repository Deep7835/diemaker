How to Make a Sticker Sheet with Step and Repeat in CorelDraw

TutorialCorelDraw5 min readUpdated 20 October 2026

One sticker on an A3 sheet is a waste of vinyl. Twenty-five stickers in tidy rows is a profitable print run. The fastest way to get there in CorelDraw is Step and Repeat, a command that copies an object a set number of times with exact spacing.

Here's the good news: once your first sticker is finished, filling the sheet takes less than a minute. Let me show you how to set it up so the gaps, bleed and cut lines all line up.

Before you start: finish one sticker

Step and Repeat copies exactly what you select, so set up one sticker completely first:

  • The artwork, with bleed running past the cut line.
  • The cut line in the CutContour spot colour, 0.25 pt, no fill.
  • Group the artwork and its cut line (Ctrl + G) so they move together.

Not sure about bleed? The bleed and safe zone guide has the numbers.

Plan the sheet first

Decide three numbers before you copy anything:

NumberTypical valueWhy
Sheet margin10 mm (0.4 in) or moreRoom for the cutter's registration marks
Gap between cut linesAt least twice the bleed, e.g. 4 to 6 mmSo neighbouring bleeds don't overlap
Rows × columnsWhatever fitsWork it out from sheet size and sticker size

Roland's GS2-24 manual, for example, recommends side margins of about 25 mm (0.98 in) when the material feeds a long way, so check your cutter's guide for its margins.

Step 1: Make the first row

  1. Select your grouped sticker.
  2. Open Edit, then Step and Repeat (Ctrl + Shift + D in most versions).
  3. Under Horizontal settings, choose Spacing between objects as the mode and type your gap in the Distance box.
  4. Under Vertical settings, choose No offset.
  5. Type the number of copies and click Apply.

Corel's help describes exactly this combination for distributing copies horizontally. One thing to watch: the number of copies doesn't include the original. A forum user notes that for 5 stickers in a row you enter 4. That's easy to verify on your first try.

Step 2: Make the rows

  1. Select the whole first row.
  2. In Step and Repeat, set Horizontal to No offset.
  3. Set Vertical to Spacing between objects, type the same gap, and choose the direction (down).
  4. Enter the number of extra rows and click Apply.

Spacing between objects vs offset

This is where it gets interesting. Step and Repeat has two ways to space copies:

  • Spacing between objects measures the gap between the edges of the copies. This is the one you want for stickers, because the gap stays the same no matter how big the sticker is.
  • Offset measures from one copy's position to the next, so it includes the sticker's own width. Corel's tutorial notes positive values move right or up and negative values move left or down.

Step 3: Centre the grid on the page

  1. Select everything (Ctrl + A) and group it.
  2. Press P to centre it on the page.
  3. Check that every sticker sits inside your margin.

Step 4: Keep cut lines on top

Before you export, make sure all cut lines sit above the artwork, ideally on their own layer. The cut line should be the top object, set to overprint, so it never knocks a gap into the print. Paperlust's setup guide asks for the cut line on the top layer, set to overprint.

What this means for you

Step and Repeat turns one careful sticker into a full sheet without any of the copies drifting out of line. The gap you type is the gap you get, which keeps bleeds apart and cuts clean.

In my view, the habit worth building is this: finish and group one sticker, plan the margin and gap, then repeat. Once you've done it this way, you'll never drag copies around by eye again.

The takeaway

To make a sticker sheet with Step and Repeat in CorelDraw, group one finished sticker, use Spacing between objects for the row, repeat the row downwards, centre the grid, and keep cut lines on top. A full, evenly spaced sheet in under a minute.

Questions people ask

What is the shortcut for Step and Repeat in CorelDraw?

Ctrl + Shift + D opens Step and Repeat in most versions. You'll also find it under the Edit menu.

How much gap should I leave between stickers?

At least twice your bleed, so neighbouring bleeds don't overlap. With 2 mm bleed, a 4 to 6 mm gap is a safe start.

Should I use Offset or Spacing between objects?

Spacing between objects. It keeps the gap between stickers the same no matter how big each sticker is.
