Sticker Bleed and Safe Zone: Simple Rules for Clean Cuts

GuideGeneral6 min readUpdated 20 October 2026

You open the box of freshly cut stickers and something looks off. On a few of them, a thin white sliver runs along one edge. On others, a letter sits so close to the edge that it's half cut away. Both problems have the same cure: the right sticker bleed and safe zone.

This is simpler than it sounds. Two numbers, set once, and your stickers come out clean. Let me show you exactly how much you need and how to check it.

Why slivers and clipped text happen

Cutters are precise, but not perfect. Paperlust, an Australian print shop, says its cutting equipment is accurate to plus or minus 0.5 mm (about 0.02 in). That small tolerance is why every printer asks for a margin on both sides of the cut line.

Here's the thing. If your artwork stops exactly at the cut line, even a tiny shift shows the blank material underneath. If your text sits right on the line, the same shift clips it.

What bleed is

Bleed is extra artwork that runs past the cut line. It's printed but cut away. Its only job is to make sure no blank edge shows if the cut lands slightly outside the line.

For a sticker with a white border, the bleed is simply more white. For an edge-to-edge sticker, you extend the background colours past the cut.

How much bleed you need

Most printers land on about 3 mm (1/8 in). Paperlust calls 3 mm beyond the cut path on all sides the standard bleed for die cut stickers in Australia. PrintKeg asks for at least 0.125 in (3.2 mm) of bleed.

In practice, 2 to 3 mm (0.08 to 0.12 in) works for most print and cut jobs. If your printer gives a number, use theirs.

TipIn CorelDraw, turn on View, then Bleed. The freecdr.in guide suggests checking that the artwork runs past the die line, not just past the page edge.

What the safe zone is

The safe zone is a margin inside the cut line. Anything important, like text, logos, phone numbers and QR codes, should sit inside it.

PrintKeg asks for text and key design elements to stay at least 0.125 in (3.2 mm) inside the cut line. Paperlust asks for at least 2 mm (0.08 in). So a safe zone of 2 to 3 mm covers most printers, and 3 mm is the safer pick.

What this means for you: decorative edges can go right up to the cut. Words and logos shouldn't.

Corners and thin parts

Bleed and safe zone get the headlines, but corners cause plenty of quiet problems too.

StickerGiant's support page says its tightest corner is a 0.04 in (about 1 mm) radius, with a default of 0.125 in (3.2 mm). One printing.com shop guide sets 3 mm (0.12 in) as the minimum corner radius on a die cut. StickerApp rounds corners for durability and warns that narrow sections can tear or lift.

So keep inside corners rounded by at least 1 mm, and avoid skinny necks between parts of the shape. If a bridge is thinner than about 2 mm (0.08 in), widen it.

Resolution and colour mode

A perfect cut can't save a blurry print. PrintKeg asks for raster artwork of at least 300 DPI at the final print size, and files in CMYK colour mode.

A quick check: a 75 mm (2.95 in) sticker at 300 DPI needs an image about 886 pixels wide. If your logo is 400 pixels wide, it will print at about 135 DPI at that size, which looks soft.

Set it up step by step

  1. Decide the final sticker size, for example 75 mm (2.95 in) wide.
  2. Draw or generate the cut line with a gap of 2 to 3 mm around the design.
  3. Extend the artwork (or white border) 2 to 3 mm past the cut line for bleed.
  4. Draw a guide 3 mm inside the cut line and keep all text inside it.
  5. Round inside corners to at least 1 mm and widen any thin bridges.
  6. Check the image is 300 DPI at the final size, then export your PDF with spot colours kept.

Once you do this once, it becomes second nature.

The takeaway

Good sticker bleed and safe zone settings are small numbers that make a big difference: about 3 mm of bleed outside the cut, about 3 mm of safe zone inside it, rounded corners, and a 300 DPI image. Set them once, check them on every file, and those white slivers and clipped letters become a thing of the past.

Questions people ask

How much bleed does a sticker need?

About 3 mm (1/8 in) is standard. Paperlust uses 3 mm and PrintKeg asks for at least 0.125 in. Always check your printer's spec.

What is a safe zone on a sticker?

It's a margin inside the cut line where important text and logos stay. Most printers ask for 2 to 3 mm (about 0.08 to 0.125 in).

Do white border stickers need bleed?

Yes. The bleed is simply more white past the cut, so a small cutting shift doesn't show the material underneath.
