How it works

From image to cutter in three steps

  1. Upload your design

    Use a PNG with a transparent background, or a JPG on a plain background. Got a full sheet of white-bordered stickers? Choose A sheet of stickers and every sticker is found for you.

  2. Set gap, shape and bleed

    Choose a contour, rectangle, rounded, circle or oval cut. Set the final size in mm or inches, a white border or extended colours, and fill a whole sheet if you need one.

  3. Import into CorelDraw

    Download the PDF and import it. The artwork and the CutContour line arrive on separate layers, ready for your RIP or die maker.

Real spot colour

The cut line is a named Separation colour, stroked, unfilled and set to overprint. It isn't plain magenta, so the RIP recognises it.

Bleed and safe zone

A white border or extended edge colours run past the cut, and a dashed safe-zone line shows where text and logos are safe.

Cutter-friendly paths

Fitted bezier curves with as few nodes as possible, all smooth, so the blade doesn't stop and turn. Rounded inside corners, and narrow areas under 2 mm flagged before you print.

White ink for clear vinyl

Add an RDG_WHITE layer under the whole sticker or the design only, for clear, transparent and metallic material.

Kiss cut + through cut

Add a second cut through the backing around each sticker or around the whole sheet. It gets its own spot colour and layer.

Exact sizes

Set the final sticker size and the artwork scales to fit around your chosen gap. It warns you when the image is under 150 dpi.

Gang-up sheets

Fill A4, A3, A3+, 12×18 or 13×19 sheets with copies, with your chosen margin and gap between cuts.

Whole sticker sheets

Upload a designed sheet of stickers with white borders. Every sticker gets its own cut line, lined up with your sheet. All of it runs in your browser.

Every file format

PDF with layers for CorelDraw, editable SVG, EPS and DXF cut files, a 300 dpi PNG, or everything in one ZIP.

CorelDraw guide

Using the file in CorelDraw

The PDF has two layers: Artwork for the CMYK print, and the cut line in its own spot colour. That's the setup print-and-cut RIPs and die makers expect.

1 · Import

  1. Choose File → Import (Ctrl+I) and select the downloaded PDF. You can also use File → Open.
  2. If asked, choose Import text as curves.
  3. Press Enter or click once to place it. Don't drag, or the size changes.
  4. Open the Objects docker and keep the cut line on top of the artwork.

2 · Check the cut line

  • Select the line and press Alt+Enter: Fill = None, outline = spot colour CutContour.
  • Line width: 0.25 pt or a hairline. Only change it if your printer asks.
  • Check it's one closed path per piece with the Shape tool (F10). The tool keeps nodes few and smooth, so the cutter runs without stopping.
  • Right-click the line and confirm Overprint Outline is ticked.
  • Turn on View → Bleed and confirm the artwork runs past the cut line.
  • White ink layer: check the shape uses RDG_WHITE and that Overprint Fill is ticked.
  • Through cut: check it uses its own spot colour, different from the kiss cut.

3 · Send to print

  1. File → Publish to PDF → Settings → Colour: untick Convert spot colours to CMYK. If the colour gets converted, the RIP can't find the cut line.
  2. Keep the artwork in CMYK.
  3. Reopen the PDF and confirm the line is still a spot colour.
  4. Tell your printer whether you want a kiss cut or a through cut.

Questions

Frequently asked

Still stuck? The guides walk through every step in CorelDraw and Photoshop.

Can I download a CorelDraw .cdr file?

Not directly. CDR is CorelDraw's own closed format: there's no public specification, and even open-source tools like Inkscape and LibreOffice can only read it, not write it. The PDF is the reliable route. In CorelDraw choose File → Open, pick the PDF, then File → Save As → CorelDRAW (.cdr). The artwork and the cut line come in as separate objects, and the cut line is a vector path in the CutContour spot colour. Check it in Object Properties before you save. SVG, EPS and DXF also open in CorelDraw as editable vectors.

Can the artwork be vector too?

Yes, for flat-colour designs. Under Artwork style, choose Vector shapes. The tool reduces the artwork to its main colours, ignores blends along edges, and traces each colour as smooth filled curves. The PDF, SVG and EPS then contain no picture at all: every shape is editable in CorelDraw. It works best on logos, text and solid-colour cartoons. Photos and 3D-shaded artwork come out blotchy, so keep those as an image (the printer prints images perfectly; only the cut line needs to be a vector).

Why are there so few nodes on the cut line?

On purpose. Every node makes a cutter or plotter slow down, so the tool fits the fewest smooth bezier curves that stay within a fraction of a millimetre of the traced edge. Neighbouring curves share one tangent, so the blade never meets a sharp turn. Raise Smoothness for even fewer nodes; on sticker sheets, choose Smoother or Smoothest under Outline. In CorelDraw you can also select the line with the Shape tool (F10), select all nodes and click Smooth to mark them as smooth nodes.

Can I cut a whole sheet of stickers at once?

Yes. Under Image, choose A sheet of stickers. The tool keeps your sheet exactly as designed (background, title and logo included) and adds a cut line just inside each sticker's white border, 0.5 mm in by default so a small cutting shift never shows the background. It works best when every sticker has a white border, and it reads the paper size (A4, 12×18, 13×19 or Letter) from the image's proportions.

Are my images uploaded anywhere?

No. Everything happens in your browser: tracing, layout and building the PDF. Your artwork never leaves your computer, and the tool works offline once the page has loaded.

Which spot colour name should I use?

Roland VersaWorks looks for a spot colour named exactly CutContour, with matching capitals, and PerfCutContour for a perforated cut. Other RIPs and die makers may use another name. Ask them, then choose Custom name and type it exactly.

What is the difference between a kiss cut and a through cut?

A kiss cut goes through the sticker only and leaves the backing intact. A through cut goes through both the sticker and the backing. Both use the same cut line; you tell your printer the cut depth.

Why is my cut line drawn around the whole image?

The tool couldn't tell the design from the background. Open Background removal, click Pick from image, then click the background in the preview. Raise the colour tolerance if bits of background are still included.

My design has separate pieces. Can I get one outline?

Yes. Increase Join nearby pieces or the gap until the outlines merge. To drop small stray bits, use Largest piece only or raise Ignore specks.

How do I print on clear, transparent or metallic vinyl?

Turn on White ink layer. The PDF gets an extra layer filled with the spot colour RDG_WHITE, which Roland VersaWorks prints in white ink under the colours. Choose The whole sticker for a solid white base, or The design only so the clear areas stay clear. Other printers may use a different name; type it in the spot colour field.

Can I get a kiss cut and a through cut in one file?

Yes. Under Cut lines, choose a Through cut: a rounded rectangle around each sticker, or one around the whole sheet. The kiss cut stays on CutContour and the through cut gets its own spot colour and layer (ThroughCut by default). Ask your printer which name their cutter uses for a through cut.

What are the orange areas in the preview?

They're parts of the sticker narrower than 2 mm, such as thin bridges or sharp notches. They can tear, lift or cut badly. Increase the gap, Round inside corners or Join nearby pieces until the orange disappears.

Should the line be 0.25 pt or a hairline?

Most RIPs read the colour name, not the width, so 0.25 pt is a safe default that's easy to see. Some die makers ask for a hairline; set the width to 0 for that.

Can I make folding box or carton dielines with this?

This tool makes outline cuts for stickers, labels, tags and badges. For folding cartons, start from the box manufacturer's dieline template, which includes the fold lines.

Does the PDF also work in Illustrator or with my cutter's software?

Yes. The cut line is a standard PDF Separation colour on its own layer (optional content group), so Illustrator, Acrobat and most RIPs see it as a named spot colour.
